## What Doodle is

A recursively zoomable creative document. On the field it is a node graph — Model → Prompt / Negative → Image Generator → Preview — in The Soft Machine. Every node can be **entered**: a written thing (page, scene, note) becomes a **document**; a **place** (a chapter) becomes a **field of what it holds**. So a book is chapters, a chapter is a field of pages, a page is a sheet you write on with beats and notes inside — and **zoom crosses the levels** (`WRITTEN`/`PLACES` in `kinds.ts`; `ENTER_AT 2.2`, `LEAVE_AT 0.18` in `Canvas.tsx`; one level per gesture). Four base workflows are templates: **Images, Film, Manga, Book**. The agent (ChatGPT via Codex, Ollama, or a mock) proposes; the user keeps.

## Decisions that hold

- Vite + React + TypeScript in front of a Tauri 2 crate. Port 1430 (Orb is 1420).
- Our own canvas engine, no React Flow: a world layer under floating chrome — moved by a `translate`, grown by CSS **`zoom`** (`.world-scale`), not `scale()`, so cards re-lay out at the magnification and type and hairlines stay crisp — SVG wires in world units.
- The graph is JSON in a folder — `Name.doodle/graph.json` + `assets/` + `backups/` + `jobs.json`. SQLite only when objects/relationships/search need it.
- The work is in-window pills; the boring is on the native menu bar (`src-tauri/src/menu.rs` → ids → `src/platform/menu.ts`). That listener belongs to the **window**, not to a component (kept on `window.__doodleMenu`, never removed): a long run of hot patches over `App` once left the platform's own menu talking to nobody, and ⌘N looked broken when nothing was.
- Providers behind one port (`src/providers/types.ts`): **mock** (real infrastructure — latency, steps, cancel, streaming, a fixed shot list, the fixture varied by seed), **Ollama** (text; available only with a writer-family model — `qwen3:14b` is installed), **ChatGPT through Codex** (text on the app server, images through `codex exec` and the CLI's image tool). Who draws / who writes is chosen on the Queue chevron and remembered (`ui.drawWith / writeWith`); node selects name checkpoints/models only.
- Signal is **Rams amber** — an orange-yellow, `--signal-hsl: 39 90% 51%` (`#f4a41a`) — on the play, on every wire (`hsl(var(--signal-hsl) / var(--wire))`) and on a connected port's dot: what is wired is what will run. **Roles** (Doodle's amendment to doctrine 8): one dusty hue per kind in the amber's own Braun register (chroma 12–50%, strength from the wash `0.22/0.36`, not the hue) — model slate, scene blue, note sand, shot clay, character rose, location khaki, style violet, generator teal, writer indigo, preview/page olive — `--role-*: h s l` in `app.css`, worn by `.k-<kind>` (`--role`) and `.t-<type>` (`--flow`: model slate, text blue, image green). Always a ground (a wash, a dot, a wire, a tile), never type, never state. Dark default; light must hold. Icon: DD in Silkscreen on charcoal.
- Class names: `.field`, `.doc`, `.page`, `.sec`, `.head`, `.sheet` are taken (system or shell) — `.sheet` cost a card its `position: absolute`. Check `soft-machine.css` and `app.css` before naming; the canvas surface is `.stage` (`.stage.reading` for a page).

## Since M2.1: the chapter is the manuscript (D1)

**Read this before the older passages below, which still say "a chapter is a field of pages".** Since format 2 (2026-09-24) a **chapter holds its own words** (`chapter.data.text`, Markdown, with `summary` its line) and is entered as a **document** — the whole chapter in one writer, its beats tied to its words and listed under it, *‹ One · 1 of 2 · Two ›* and **+ New chapter** at its foot; `PLACES` is empty (the book's root is the only field; the set waits for the inspiration board). **Pages are a layout**, never cards: the chapter card on the field lists the pages its words lay out into (`paginate()`, ~350 words each) — how each opens, where it falls, *1 page · 301 words*; Read runs each chapter's words on in order (`sectionWords`); a writer wired into a chapter **adds** its words after the chapter's own (never over them), streaming shown where they will land; *Continue* on a chapter reads how the chapter before ends. Loose `page` cards (not in a chapter) still exist and still paginate onto their siblings. **The migration** (`joinChapters` in `integrity.ts`, format 1 → 2, run on open): each chapter's pages in their order become its words, one paragraph break apart; what was inside a page (beats, notes) moves into the chapter, and a beat tied to a page's words is re-tied to the same words in the chapter (offset by where the page now begins in the chapter's plain words); a page's pictures become the chapter's; wires to or from a page are to or from its chapter (one wire into a chapter's words); the notice says so and the file as it was is kept as `graph-before-<secs>.json` (a copy the mending or migrating kept; `graph-damaged-…` only when it could not be read). Checked on the benchmark book: 360 pages into 30 chapters, **133,336 words before and after**, each chapter its pages in order, 90 beats moved; unit-tested with a beat inside a page's bold words. Tidy Unused Pictures now also counts what backups and kept copies name. **The manuscript** (M2.2, `canvas/Read.tsx`, the head's book key — now labelled *Manuscript*): every chapter in one column, each a live writer; a capsule at the top of the column names the chapter in view (`useHere`: the last heading past a third of the way down) and opens a list of all of them — picking one scrolls it to the top (`scroll-margin-top`; the column keeps 70vh below the last chapter so any can come to the top). Measured on the 134k-word book in release: opens in ~350 ms, scrolls at 60 fps, a keystroke mid-book lands inside one frame — so nothing is virtualised; Measure Performance keeps watching (*open a chapter*, *manuscript opens … scrolls … a keystroke lands in a … frame*). **Pages as layout** (M2.3): a chapter's card lists the pages its words lay out into and reflows as they change; `paginate` is deterministic, so a page's number is stable for a given text; the PDF's own layout is M7. **Focus** (M2.4; View › Focus, ⇧⌘F, ⌘K; Escape leaves): only while writing — a chapter, a page, the manuscript (elsewhere it says so). The head, panes, foot, bar, the page's meta line, its turn and what is below it, the manuscript's jump key and the block key in the margin **fade** (opacity, never moved or unmounted, so the words stay where they were and the caret is never lost), the panes' padding goes so the column centres, and the caret's line is held **two fifths down** as you write (`typewrite()` in `Editor.tsx`, on every change of words or caret, and on the way in). The paragraph with the caret is lit and the rest step back (`.here`, a node decoration on the caret's top-level block; only the writer that has the caret, `.ProseMirror-focused`) — Settings › General › Writing: *Dim the rest* / *All lit* (`ui.dim`, remembered; `ui.focusing` never is). Leaving the words leaves Focus. **Goals and stats** (M2.5; `state/stats.ts` + test, `shell/Goal.tsx`): while writing, the foot's readouts give way to a **ring** and *412 today* (*412 / 500* with a goal); opened: today, this chapter or page, the book (words · reading time at 230 a minute), days in a row, and the daily goal (a stepper, 250 at a time; 0 is none — `saveSoon()` writes it, as it is not a journal entry). **Today is the net change** in the book's words (chapters and pages, rejected ones not) since the day's first look, never below nothing; the ledger (`stats.days`, by local date, the newest 400) lives in `graph.json`, is noted a second after typing pauses and once a minute (one timer for the window, `__doodleCount`), and turns over at local midnight; a streak counts back from today, or from yesterday while today is under way. A document's children are split: **Beats** (notes and shots, written in the list) and **Inside** (a brief, a writer, a generator — a row each that opens it). **Find and replace** (M2.6; Edit › Find and Replace…, ⌘F, ⌘K; `state/find.ts` + test, `shell/Find.tsx`, `state/reveal.ts`): a card beside the words (not a sheet over them), staying while you go from hit to hit. It looks through every written thing *as written* (Markdown and all, so replacing never loses a mark): chapters' words, titles and lines, pages, beats and notes, briefs, and the names, descriptions and titles of people, places, styles and shots — rejected cards left be. **Aa** (case), **Whole words** (Unicode letters), **.\*** (a pattern; `$1`… in the replacement; a pattern that is not one, or matches nothing, says so). Hits by card, in the book's order, each in context: one goes to its words (enters the card, or in the manuscript stays) and **selects** them — `revealAt(node, start, end)` in raw offsets; `Prose` turns them into plain-word offsets for `Editor`'s `reveal`. **Replace all** is one journal entry (⌘Z puts every one back) and says how many; **Replace** does one; a beat tied to changed words is re-tied to the words as replaced. An empty chapter's card says *Not begun*. **Comments** (M2.7; `state/comments.ts`, `canvas/Margin.tsx`): a remark on a passage, **in the margin** — a `comment` node (new kind, no ports) inside what it is about, tied to its words by the same anchors as beats (so it stays with them through edits around them; *Its words are gone from here* when they go). Made from the selection bubble (**Comment**, beside **Beat**) or Edit › Add Comment (⌥⌘M, on the words last chosen — `picked`, kept past the blur a menu causes). Its passage wears the tint (`.tie.remark`), a quieter mark than a beat's sand underline; the card lights it on hover. Cards stand in the margin beside the column, level with their words and pushed down so none covers another — **if there is room to the next thing beside the column** (the Inspector when it is out, else the window; `ROOM` 268): otherwise a small mark at the column's edge opens the card. The margin looks again when the panes or Focus change (they move the column without resizing it). **Resolve** puts it away (its mark goes too); *n resolved* shows them faded, to reopen; × deletes. Comments are never the book's words: not in Read's text, export, word counts, Find, the Outline or a document's lists. The card's class is `.comment-card` — `.remark` is already the passage's (Playwright found the clash). **Spelling** (M2.8; `spell.rs` + test, `writer/spell.ts`, `state/lexicon.ts` + test): **the Mac's own checker, asked by Doodle rather than by WebKit** — WebKit's cannot be told a book's words without writing to the system-wide list. Rust calls `NSSpellChecker` (`objc2-app-kit`, macOS only) per paragraph — `checkSpellingOfString:startingAt:language:wrap:NO…` in a loop (the short form **wraps round to the start**), UTF-16 offsets as JavaScript counts — and `guessesForWordRange…` for suggestions. The page asks a paragraph at a time, what it said kept by the paragraph's words (only a changed paragraph is asked again, gathered 350 ms after typing pauses), and draws `.misspelt` (the Mac's red dots — the one colour that is not ours) for words neither the Mac nor **the book** knows: every name of its people, places, styles and shots (by their parts, and possessives — `lexicon()`), and the words taught to it (**right-click a marked word**: the Mac's guesses, *Add "…" to the book's words* — kept in `graph.json` → `words` — or *Ignore it while this is open*). The writer's own `spellcheck` is off when Doodle checks (else on). Settings › Writing › Spelling: Check / Off. Outside the app nothing is marked. The Mac itself passes some common typos (*frist*, *teh*). Checked in the real window: *lihgt* and *Quillon* marked, *Mara* not; Quillon taught, unmarked. (Driving it: re-fronting the window between the right-click and the choice dismissed the menu — click straight after.) **Not yet:** a recovery log written before a migration replays pages back in (only after a crash on the old build).

## How it works, by area

**Window.** Overlay title bar, lights at `{20,32}` (rows centre on y 30). Head: `‹ tab ›` — the arrows step to the previous/next **sibling of the same kind** of the entered node (`nav.siblings/sibling/step`, View › Previous/Next, ⇧⌘[ ⇧⌘]) — with the trail when entered (`Untitled film › Scene 08`), the **Read** key (a book, on a place only), the amber **Queue** split pill (run · chevron menu), `×` stop, duplicate, `≡` panes. Panes float as cards at content height: **Outline** (`shell/Navigator.tsx`: the whole document as a tree the way Blender's outliner shows a scene — the book, then chapters, pages, what they hold; twist folds; the way down to here is always unfolded; click goes there — that level shown, it selected and framed; double-click enters; click a chosen row again to **rename** in place; **drag** a row before/after another or into it (`graph.reorder`: order is `seq`, siblings renumbered; a new parent gives it a spot to the right on that field); `+` adds under what is chosen — a chapter at the book, a page in a chapter, a beat in a scene, a note in a page; rejected rows dim; the tree takes the **keyboard** when clicked into: arrows walk, fold and unfold and go up, **⌘↑ ⌘↓** move a row among its own, ⏎ enters, F2 renames) and **Inspector** (state segment; the node's fields from the kind registry; the **Bible** — tone/rules/avoid — when nothing is selected at the root). Foot: **you** — a round mark (`boring-avatars`, bauhaus, seeded `WHO` in `Foot.tsx`, a placeholder until a picture is set) → Settings (General: shortcuts, motion; Appearance: light/dark/system; Providers: who is up; About), readouts `T I N`, the view cluster (fit · **the map** · lens │ − % +). The bar at the foot is one plate: a mono line naming whose words and where they go (`Scene 08 → Storyboard`, the role's dot), the words growing to four lines, then quiet keys (runs, save), the note, and one round amber key — run, or stop while it runs; on a page it is the **ask** with the same key. On the field **Enter runs and puts the bar away** (`ui.bar`, never remembered; × puts it away too), shift-Enter breaks a line; away, it is one quiet round key at the foot (`.bar-key`), and **/** or the key brings it back focused with the caret at the end (also ⌘K → The prompt bar). On a page the ask is the same key until opened (`ui.ask`, `/` or the key; Escape or × closes). A chapter's field gets the prompt bar for its own first runnable. `nav.reading(focus, read)` is the one answer to "is this a document?". **Read** (`canvas/Read.tsx`, the head's book key on a place, `ui.read`, never remembered; Escape leaves it first; it puts the bar away): a place — the book, a chapter — as one column: title, then chapter by chapter, page by page in order, every page a `Prose` written in where it lies with its name as a folio.

**Field** (`canvas/Canvas.tsx`). Pan (trackpad, Space-drag, middle), pinch/⌘-wheel zoom (in past 2.2 over a written thing enters it; out past 0.18 inside something rises with the thing left framed; on a document a pinch out rises), fixed zoom stops, fit between the panes, marquee, shift-select, drag with 8 px snap on drop, arrows nudge, ⌥-drop into a card, right-click menus (node: Open/Run/Duplicate/State/Delete; field: Add here/Fit), double-click empty → Add here, `›` open key on hover, Enter/Escape enter and rise, L or the cluster key = lens (unrelated fades), below 0.4 zoom = the **far view** (`.lod-map`: title-only cards, ports on the band; `setMapMode` in `layout.ts`). **The map** (`shell/Minimap.tsx`, the cluster's eye or **M**, remembered): this level whole in the corner — every card its role's wash, the window's own view a slab of tint; press or drag on it to look there.

**Cards** (`canvas/Node.tsx`, `layout.ts`). Read back against the doctrine, not against a screenshot (see *Taste* below); drawn as the platform would draw its own objects in The Soft Machine's terms — the system's lit lip and fallen shade, lifted by a two-light shadow (`--card-lift`, `--card-lift-2`: a close contact and a long soft fall), the name at the headline size (13/600). **Head:** a band in the role's wash, a little brighter at the top than the bottom, a **lit dot** (`--lit-*`, the roles at full chroma, worn only by small round marks — a card's dot, the bar's, a page's meta, a lineage row, the map's selection) and the name; the **output** is one **jack** on the band's right edge (`HEAD_H 36`). State is a sentence-case word in a capsule of tint (*Draft*). **Inputs:** 26 px rows (`PORT_ROW 26`) from `ROWS_TOP 42` down the left edge — a jack, the port's name, and *what feeds it* as a **token**: a capsule in the source's own wash with its lit dot, the way a variable sits in a sentence. `layout.ts` and the CSS share these numbers; when they drifted (24 vs 26) the plugs hung above their jacks. A **jack** is a ring cut into the card's edge, dark, with a thin collar in what flows through it (`.t-<type>`: model slate, text blue, image green); patched, the collar is amber. Ports carry `data-port="node:port"` / `data-dir`. **Body:** what the card is — prompts and notes a slot of words that grows with them (`field-sizing: content`, to 260 px), a model a pop-up, a **preview** its print to the card's edges and corners (*Nothing yet* until there is one), a **character** a contact card (the face in a round, or their initials on their role until there is one), a **location** a map card (the view across the top, 16:9, the role's hatch until there is a picture), a generator ends in its bloom of takes; the kinds with settings (generate, write, style, shot) show **the fields the kind declares, live** (`Form` → the inspector's own `FieldRow`: one registry, two places). **Nothing on a card is drawn** (doctrine 1): no row plates, no dividers, no rule under a group's name — a group is a word (`.node-rule`) with `--gap-near` above it. Labels Inter, figures mono (doctrine 7). Controls keep the capsule and wear the **tint** when chosen (doctrine 5): a **slider** is a thin track filled in the role's lit tone with the platform's round white knob and its figure beside it (`-webkit-appearance: none` on the input and the thumb, or WebKit draws its own); a **segmented** choice is a track of tint with the chosen segment the card's own face lifted out of it; a stepper is the same track with the figure between its keys; a chooser is a **pop-up** — its word and the up-and-down chevrons (`PopUpIcon`, here and in the inspector); something written into is a slot. A row whose control will not fit beside its name gives it the line below, against the far edge. On a card everything is **flat**; the tactile versions of the same controls live in the panes.

**Wires are patch leads** (`Wires.tsx`): amber, **hanging** between their ends (`wirePath` sags with the run, to 30 px, flat in the far view), each end a **plug** seated in its jack (`.wire-plug`); the cables feeding a running node carry a marching dash (`.wire.working`, still for reduced motion). A wire that **crosses a level** is drawn as far as it can be seen: dashed into the band of the card that holds its other end (`resolve()` climbs to the card at this level), or as a short dashed **lead** named in mono (`.wire-from`) when it comes from beyond this level; both ends inside one card = not drawn. While a wire is out, landable inputs light and it snaps to the nearest within 40 px; let go on the field → `Offer` (one row per kind that takes it, made there, wired); **C** connects two selected; double-click cuts.

**Kinds** (`graph/kinds.ts`): model, prompt, generate, preview, character, **location**, style, write, page, note, shot, chapter — ports, a starting size, default data, the fields (`line`, `text`, `select`, `number`, `range`, `choice`, `seed`). A node can also be **given inputs by hand** (`node.extras`, `graph.addInput/dropInput`): right-click a writer or a generator → **Give it** → a character, a place, a style, a scene, a note, a shot — the card is made to its left, the port appears, the wire is drawn, and what it holds rides into every request (`given()` in `jobs.ts`); *Tidy the empty ones* takes back the ports nothing feeds. A card is **as tall as what it holds**: nothing sets a height, the kinds that would collapse carry a `min-height` on their body in `app.css`, and a `ResizeObserver` tells the engine what the browser laid out (`graph.measure`, `offsetHeight`, which CSS `zoom` does not scale — no journal entry, no dirty document) so bounds, fits, marquees and drops agree with the screen. **Width** is set by hand: the grip on a card's right edge (`.node-grip`, double-click restores the kind's own), journalled and coalesced per card. A **page** and a **chapter** are **paper** on the field (`.node.leaf`: `--paper`, a hairline `--paper-edge` as a cut sheet — the one edge on content, deliberate; a **running head** instead of a band — *Page 1  from Write*, small, in Inter — with the one input on the head's left edge (`portPos`) and no input rows; 20 px margins; the foot carries the count and the **folio**, the page's place among its own): the page is its words, written on in place at 13.5/1.7, 300×400; the chapter is a sheet with **two more stacked under it** (box-shadows, `--paper-under`), its name at the headline weight, its summary, and its **contents** the way a book lists them — each page's name, how it opens, its number — five, then *and n more*, and the count. A chapter's `text` output is its pages in order (`describe()`); its prose key is `summary`. The navigator's `+` inside a chapter lays the next page to the right. Templates in `graph/templates.ts` (the Film's Storyboard has a `place` port given by hand, fed by *The greenhouse*, laid under the Model so it clears the style card — cards are as tall as they hold, so a template spaces for the grown height, not the kind's `size.h`; the Book is a cast, a voice and two chapters of pages). Every node has `parent` (entering), `seq` (creation order), `status` (canon / draft / exploration / rejected — rejected never counts as context, wears the word, steps back).

**Pages** (`canvas/Doc.tsx`). Entered, a written node is a page — the writing mode: the stage takes a calmer ground (`--reading`), one column at a 680 px measure set between the panes (`.win.nav-on/.ins-on` pad the `.docpage`), a mono meta line with the role's dot (`PAGE · IN ONE · 37 WORDS`, Run for a runnable), a **Prose | Screenplay** switch (`data.form`), and ≈ running time for a screenplay (180 words a page, a page a minute), the title inline at 32 px (renames), the words at 16.5/1.75 in **the writer** (below) with the caret already in them at the end; a page ends in its **turn** (`Turn`: ‹ previous · n of N · next ›, or **+ New page** on the last, made beside it and entered); what it holds and its media wait below as quiet one-line sections (`.paper-below`, the `+` on hover). Then its body — prose for prompt/note/page; character sheet; style sheet (palette as chips); generator = contact sheet of takes (ringed = the take) + settings + runs + its own Run; preview = the print; writer = output + settings; shot = description + Camera group; then **Beats/Notes** (children as a numbered list written in place, `›` opens) and **Media**. `@` in prose opens the graph's names (the writer's menu, fed by `mentionables()` in `canvas/mentions.tsx`), and a name after `@` reads as a token; `expandMentions` turns `@Title` into the thing's description in every request. Select words → the bubble's **+ Beat** (**+ Note** on a page), and the child is **tied** to the passage (`state/anchors.ts`: the anchor is the quoted words plus the offset they were taken at in the **plain** words — `plain()`, the markup read away — never an index, so bolding a passage never loosens its tie). `locate()` finds them again to the letter, then ignoring how the whitespace fell, then by their first and last four words — so a tie survives inserting before it and editing inside it, and says **adrift** when the passage is gone rather than pointing somewhere wrong. The writer marks tied passages under the words (decorations, `.tie`, a sand underline; lit when the pointer is on the beat), and a beat's row says where it came from with **untie** / **re-tie** (re-tie takes the next passage you select).

**The writer** (`src/writer/`). ProseMirror (`schema.ts`, `Editor.tsx`), in every place words are written — the page in writing mode, a page's sheet on the field, Read, a note, a prompt. **The words stay text** in `node.data.text`, so the prompts, pagination, Read, the export and anchors go on reading words: prose is a small **Markdown** (`#`/`##`/`###`, `>` quotes, `**strong**`, `_em_` — `*em*` read too, and written inside a word — `~~strike~~`, a line break inside a paragraph, a blank line between paragraphs); a screenplay is a small **Fountain** (a scene heading starts INT./EXT. or is forced with `.`, a character in capitals with dialogue on the lines under it, a parenthetical in brackets, a transition ends in TO: or is forced with `>`, the rest action; `!` forces action). `markup.ts` reads and writes both, escapes what would otherwise read as markup, and keeps a delimiter from ever touching a space on its inside (`tidy`) — the plan (§8.3) wanted ProseMirror JSON as canonical; text was chosen because Markdown and Fountain are what a writer, a model and an export already read, and it spares every consumer a second format. `pnpm test` fuzzes it: 1,000 random documents per form through write → read → write → read, the document and its words unchanged (5,000 were run once). Typing: `## ` a heading, `> ` a quote, `**…**`/`*…*`/`~~…~~` marks as you close them; ⌘B ⌘I ⇧⌘X; ⇧⌘T/H/J/B title, heading, subheading, body (Notes' own keys); ⇧↩ a line break. In a screenplay: `INT. ` makes a scene heading, Enter moves on the way the trade does (scene → action, character → dialogue, dialogue → action, transition → scene; Enter on an empty element steps back to action), Tab turns the block (action → character → transition → scene; dialogue ⇄ parenthetical), `(` in dialogue a parenthetical, `… TO:` a transition. Around the words: a **bubble** over a selection (B I S, and the page's + Beat), a quiet **key in the margin** naming the caret's block with a menu to change it (hidden beside the Outline on a narrow window), `@` tokens. The editor keeps its own **history**: Edit › Undo/Redo go to the writer with the keyboard (`editorUndo` in `platform/menu.ts`) before the journal; a change from elsewhere (a writer streaming, the journal) is read in again with the caret kept where it was in the words. An image model reads the words **bare** (`describe(n, true)` in `jobs.ts`); a writer reads them as written.

**The agent.** `state/drafts.ts`: Expand / Continue / Rewrite / free ask → a **draft** on the page, streamed word by word when the provider can (`streamText`), Stop cancels; Keep appends into the kind's words (`proseKey`; Replace for rewrite), discard drops. `state/shots.ts`: **Shots** on a scene → six under `SHOTS_SCHEMA` (Codex honours `outputSchema`; Ollama takes it as `format`; mock returns its list) → **Proposed shots** ghosts → Keep / Keep all / drop → `shot` nodes inside the scene. `bibleText()` and mentions ride into every request; rejected nodes never do.

**Doodle as an MCP server** (`src-tauri/src/mcp.rs`, `src/agent/tools.ts`, `state/ideas.ts`) — the agent works the document with tools (plan §9, §11.3: **observe and propose**, never mutate). A Streamable-HTTP MCP endpoint on `127.0.0.1:<port the system picks>/mcp` (`tiny_http`), behind a bearer token made fresh each launch (a request without it: 401); it only speaks the protocol and hands `tools/list` and `tools/call` to the webview as a `mcp` event, answered with `mcp_reply` (30 s). Codex gets it **per thread**, never through `~/.codex/config.toml`: `thread/start` → `config.mcp_servers.doodle = { url, http_headers: { Authorization }, default_tools_approval_mode: "approve", … }` plus `developerInstructions` (`briefing()`). Why `approve`: under `approvalPolicy: never` an MCP call is otherwise refused (*"MCP tool call requires approval"*); the modes are `auto | prompt | writes | approve`, found by passing a wrong one. The tools: `doodle_here` (what is open and selected, the path, the bible), `doodle_outline` (the tree with kinds, ids, states, sizes), `doodle_read` (a node whole — words as written, fields, what feeds it, what is inside, tied passages), `doodle_find`; and `propose_beats` (with `quote`: a kept beat is tied to that passage), `propose_shots` (into the shot proposals), `propose_text` (a draft, replace or append), `propose_characters`, `propose_places` — **ideas** are ghosts on the page (`state/ideas.ts`, Keep / Keep all / drop, one journal entry). A **free ask** on a page carries the tools (`drafts.ts`): the draft's head says what the agent is doing (*ChatGPT · reading…*), and when it used them its reply is an account, read and dismissed, never kept (`reply`); what it proposed waits on its own. The **mock is an agent too** — it calls the same functions directly (`req.runTool`: here → read → propose a beat per sentence, tied; *shots* or *characters* in the ask for those), so the whole loop runs for free. Ollama has no tools yet (it answers in words). One listener per window (`__doodleAgent`, the previous one gone before the next arrives — React's development double mount once left two, and every tool ran twice). Codex's reader emits `message` when the agent speaks again after a tool, so the draft shows only its latest words. Checked on 2026-09-23 with ChatGPT: *"Propose three beats for this panel"* → it read the page and proposed three beats, each quoting its passage.

**Codex** (`src-tauri/src/codex.rs`, `providers/codex.ts`). **OCR** (M3.2; `src/readers/ocr.ts`). A PDF's pages with no text layer are drawn by pdf.js at 1600 px across (`pagePictures`, one open document) and read through Ollama by the first OCR model it has: **Chandra OCR 2** (`hf.co/mradermacher/chandra-ocr-2-GGUF:Q4_K_M`, `/api/chat`, `think: false`, temperature 0 — **100% on the fixture, 2.7 s a page warm**), whose answer is layout-labelled HTML (`<div data-label="Section-Header|Text|List-Group|Table|Caption|Page-Header|Page-Footer|Picture">`) that `fromLayout` makes Markdown — headings kept, running heads, page numbers and pictures left out; else **GLM-OCR** (`glm-ocr`, `/api/generate`, "Text Recognition:", 1.5 s) which on a whole page **goes round and reads the page again until it runs out** (temperature 0 is its default; cropping and a repeat penalty do not help), so it is streamed and cut where the page's opening comes round again (`repeatsAt`) — it reads the body word for word but misses a bold title (94.5%). The import shows *Reading “x.pdf”: scanned page 3 of 40, with Chandra OCR 2…* in a notice with **Stop** (`retell` updates a notice in place). The acceptance runs in vitest and in the WebKit smoke when Ollama is up (skipped when not). **System WebKit (macOS 26) has no async iterator on `ReadableStream` and pdf.js's `getTextContent` needs one** — Playwright's WebKit has it, so only the real window showed it; `pdf.ts` shims it before pdf.js loads. Imports now log their counts (`import pdf: 1 chapter, 110 words, 1 pages, 1 read by Chandra OCR 2, 9970 ms`) and their failures.

**Import into chapters** (M2.9; `import.rs` + test, `state/importer.ts` + tests, `state/importing.ts`). Rust `read_import(path)` → `{ name, kind, text }` — Markdown, plain text and Fountain read as UTF-8 (lossy), **`.docx`** read from `word/document.xml` with `quick-xml` 0.42 (**str-based API**: names and values are `&str`; text events are plain, entities arrive as `GeneralRef` — `resolve_char_ref()` for `&#8217;`) into Markdown: headings by style (`Heading1-3`, `Title`) or `outlineLvl`, italic `_x_` and bold `**x**` with the spaces kept outside the marks, `<w:br/>` a line break, `<w:b w:val="0"/>` off; refuses other kinds with a sentence; 32 MB cap. Tested: a 30-chapter Markdown file → 30 chapters (the acceptance). **Wired** three ways, all to `importFrom(path)`: File › **Import…** (⇧⌘I, and ⌘K *Import a manuscript…*) through the dialog; a manuscript **dropped on the window** (`App.tsx`'s `onFileDrop` — pictures still go to the card under them); and a manuscript **handed over** — the command line, the Dock icon, *Open With* (`opened.rs`'s `ours` takes the extensions; `Info.plist` declares Markdown, plain text and Word as a *Viewer* at rank **Alternate**, so Doodle is offered, never the default). At launch a handed-over manuscript waits until the book is open (`launch()`), so `pnpm tauri dev -- -- <project.doodle> <book.md> <letters.docx>` is how to test it without the dialog. The chapters go **at the root**, rising there first: `placeAt` carries on the book's own grid — six to a row, 320 × 300, from its first chapter, the new ones after the ones there — if nothing stands in the way and the first new one comes after the last chapter in reading order; else a block below everything. **One journal entry** (⌘Z takes the whole import back), the new chapters selected, the whole level fitted (`fitLevel` — `fitAll` fits the selection), and a notice with the count and words. ## Working

`pnpm tauri dev` (Rust ~1 min first time; `DOODLE_CODEX_TRACE=1 pnpm tauri dev` to watch Codex); `pnpm tauri dev -- -- <path.doodle>` opens a project at launch (no dialog). `pnpm dev` alone serves the page for the browser pane and for `pnpm e2e` (which starts it if it is not running). Release for measuring, not for handing over: `pnpm tauri build --no-bundle` → run `src-tauri/target/release/doodle <path>` (it shares the installed app's Application Support and log; the dev binary's WebKit storage is `~/Library/WebKit/doodle`, the installed app's `…/com.x0bd.doodle`). The log: `~/Library/Logs/com.x0bd.doodle/doodle.log` (dev echoes it as `[log] …`).

**Driving the real window** — when the user is working in another Space (the installed app full-screen), **don't bring the dev window forward**: `wins.swift PID` lists every window of a pid on or off screen, and `screencapture -l <id>` captures the off-screen dev window by its id, no activation, no clicks; for a look at normal zoom without the real window, a throwaway Playwright test can build the graph through the page's modules (`import('/src/state/graph.ts')`, pictures as data URLs, which `urlFor` passes through). (The capture kit lives in the session scratchpad and is **lost when the scratchpad is cleared** — rebuild in a minute): `winp.swift PID` (prints `id x y w h` of that process's window, CoreGraphics), `shotp.sh PID [out] [size]` (front by pid, `screencapture -l`, downscale), `click.swift` (CGEvent move + down/up), `frontclick.sh PID X Y` (clicks **only if that pid is frontmost** — the installed app's full-screen window sits over the dev one, and a blind click once landed in it), `wins.swift PID` (every window of a pid, on or off screen), `dclick.swift X Y` (a real double-click: the second click's `mouseEventClickState` = 2 — how to enter a card), `click X Y right` (a right-click), `wk.swift <url> <out.png>` (a WKWebView snapshot of a test page — the **system WebKit**, which is how the dropped-space bug was bisected; Playwright's WebKit build does not reproduce it). Menu items by name through System Events, **by pid**: `tell (first process whose unix id is P) … click menu item "Measure Performance" of menu 1 of menu bar item "Help"`. Native alerts: the dialog plugin's three-button `message` ignores synthetic clicks — `perform action "AXPress" of button n of window 1` when it is in the AX tree; the two-button `ask` took a CGEvent click. **Never press Return on the field** (it runs the queue — ChatGPT in dev); never capture the whole screen (it holds the user's other apps) — capture the window. Drive the app read-only; leave file dialogs (open/save) to the user.

**Committing:** run `npx playwright test > /tmp/e2e.out 2>&1` and commit **only if `grep " failed" /tmp/e2e.out` finds nothing** — a `… | tail` pipeline once hid a failure and a commit went in red. For a look at the UI without the real window, a throwaway `e2e/look.e2e.ts` that does the steps and `page.screenshot({ path })` (then delete it) is quick and exact.

**The page in dev:** a hot patch of `doc.ts` (or anything it owns) leaves a fresh store beside the old graph — *Untitled* over a saved project, stale notices, a sheet that will not open — `touch index.html` for a clean page, or restart. In the browser pane, `import('/src/…')` from the console can be a **different module instance** from the running app's (Vite serves patched modules as `?t=…`) — drive through the DOM or real keys; the pane's own key presses may not reach the page (dispatch a `KeyboardEvent` on `window`). A module-level timer or listener must be kept on `window` and replaced, never added again (`__doodleMenu`, `__doodleAgent`, `__doodleCount`). React's development double mount runs effects twice — one-shot work goes through a once-per-page promise (`launch()`). The pane is 1024 px wide shown at 800: click coordinates are the screenshot's.

## Taste, as settled with the user

- **Read the doctrine, not the screenshot.** References have been welcome (a modular synth panel, the Wavely node canvas), but taken literally they were rejected twice: a flat one-tone head with a cut-in plate ("ugly"), then a tactile panel of raised keys, inset wells and plates on every card ("the tactile plate does not work on the cards"). What held was translating them through `the-soft-machine.md`: nothing drawn, distance groups, Inter reads / mono reports, the tint for a chosen thing, capsules on controls.
- **Keep:** the role band on the head, the **lit dots** (bright, not the washed roles), the **amber leads** with plugs and jacks, cards that size to what they hold, the Outline, the welcome.
- **The user reacts to what they see in the real window.** Verify there (the capture kit), in both grounds, before calling a visual change done — the browser pane has hidden two real bugs (`.col` rows outside a pane; the drag region eating clicks).
- When the user says *revert*, revert exactly to the named state and keep only what they named.
